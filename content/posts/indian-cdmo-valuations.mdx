In November 2026, PM Modi [asked Indian industry to cut its dependence on imported APIs](https://enewstime.in/national/modi-domestic-manufacturing-import-dependence-viksit-bharat-110769.html) and make them here. An API, active pharmaceutical ingredient, is the part of a medicine that actually works. The rest of the tablet is mostly filler.

That one push connects to a group of Indian stocks trading at 85x earnings or more. To see why, I went from the whole pharma industry, to contract manufacturers (CDMOs), to the major players, and then to one company, Anthem Biosciences. I use ideas from [why a high P/E can make sense](/blog/how-stock-valuations-work/). Not investment advice, and I can be wrong.

## 1. Indian pharma: strong in medicines, weak in ingredients

India is a big maker of generic medicines, copies of drugs whose patents have expired. But many of the ingredients for those medicines come from China.

- India imported about [$7.4 billion of APIs in 2025](https://upstox.com/news/business-news/latest-updates/india-s-pharma-china-problem-isn-t-over-niti-aayog-flags-heavy-api-dependence/article-195889/), per NITI Aayog. In the top categories, 65% to 86% came from China.
- Chinese APIs are about 35% to 40% cheaper. That's why the dependence built up.
- The government's incentive scheme for bulk drugs had attracted [₹5,210 crore of investment by June 2026](https://www.millenniumpost.in/amp/business/pli-scheme-for-bulk-drugs-attracts-rs-5210-cr-investment-till-june-2026-677390), with sales of about ₹3,792 crore so far.

So when Modi talks about making APIs at home, he's pointing at India's biggest weak spot in pharma.

### How the market values pharma

On 27 November 2026, [Tipsheet](https://tipsheet.markets/valuation/) showed Nifty Pharma at a P/E of 46.6, costlier than on 91% of its past days. The Nifty 50 was at 20.9. The [ten year median for Nifty Pharma is about 35](https://indexpe.in/nifty-pharma).

Pharma gets a premium because medicine demand doesn't stop in a slowdown. In 2020, when Covid hit, [Nifty Pharma rose about 61%](https://www.wrightresearch.in/stocks/index/nifty-pharma/). But safe doesn't mean cheap. Right now, pharma is priced well above its own usual.

## 2. Where CDMOs fit

The API push is mostly about generic medicines. CDMOs are the high end of the same idea.

A CDMO, contract development and manufacturing organisation, is the lab and factory behind big drug companies. The US or European drug company invents a new molecule and owns the patent. The CDMO works out how to make it, makes batches for clinical trials, and then makes the API at scale once the drug is approved.

| | Generic API maker | CDMO |
|---|---|---|
| Makes | Ingredients for off-patent drugs | Ingredients for patented, new drugs |
| Competes on | Price, mostly against China | Science, quality and reliability |
| Customer can switch | Easily | Only with years of re-approval |
| Margins | Thin | Can be high |

So the CDMO isn't directly what the API scheme pays for. But both come from the same thing: the world, and India, want to depend less on China for drug ingredients.

### Why customers stay for years

Once a CDMO is written into a drug's USFDA filing, changing it is a big headache. The new supplier must take over the process, run stability tests, prove it makes exactly the same thing, and often wait for approval. That can take [one to three years or more](https://pda.org/pda-letter-portal/home/full-article/expert-perspectives-in-km-between-sponsors-and-third-parties).

If the drug sells, supply can continue for a decade or more. At its IPO, Anthem's top customers had been with it for about 12 years on average. The flip side: if the drug fails a trial, or the customer cuts orders, that income goes.

## 3. Same industry, different stage in different countries

Contract drug making is mature in Switzerland and the US. In India, it's still early.

| | Mature: Western CDMOs | Early: Indian CDMOs |
|---|---|---|
| Example | Lonza at [about 31x expected 2026 earnings](https://www.ad-hoc-news.de/boerse/news/corporate-news/lonza-group-stock-eases-below-recent-high-as-investors-digest-guidance-and-valuation/70070174), Siegfried at [about 17x past earnings](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/SFZN.SW/) | Anthem, Divi's, Sai Life at 85x to 96x past earnings |
| Size | Lonza sold [CHF 3.4 billion in the first half of 2026](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-lonza-h1-2026-profit-gains-fail-to-lift-shares-93CH-4806064) | Anthem sold about ₹2,124 crore in all of FY26, roughly $250 million |
| Effect of one $100 million contract | About 1% more sales | About 40% more sales |
| Cost | High | A fraction, for chemists and approved plants |

That's why comparing Anthem's P/E with Lonza's tells you little. They're at different stages of the same industry.

## 4. The short term story: what's happening now

**China+1.** Drug companies want a supplier outside China, without dropping China. The US [BIOSECURE Act became law in December 2025](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2025/12/the-biosecure-act-becomes-law-in-the-united-states). It restricts US government linked business with Chinese "biotech companies of concern". The name people talk about most is WuXi AppTec, a giant Chinese contractor. But existing contracts get about five years, so the full effect lands in the early 2030s.

**It's slow so far.**
- At a global pharma fair in October 2026, [one Indian firm said it got US orders that used to go to China, while another said Europe still prefers China](https://medicaldialogues.in/news/industry/pharma/india-gains-pharma-orders-from-china-as-western-buyers-diversify-supply-chains-cphi-firms-180836).
- WuXi itself grew revenue [38.6% in January to September 2026](https://www.wuxiapptec.com/news/wuxi-news/wuxi-apptec-delivers-strong-q1-q3-2026-results-and-further-raises-full-year-guidance-driven-by-greater-success-of-multiple-customers-products-and-crdmo-model).
- In November it [came off a Pentagon list under a court order](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/11/the-double-helix-bind-wuxi-walks-off-pentagon-list).

**The latest results.** In the July to September 2026 quarter, Anthem's sales came in below one broker's estimate, but its operating margin was about 46% against about 40% expected. Management [credited a better product mix and buying less from China](https://au.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/earnings-call-transcript-anthem-biosciences-posts-strong-q2-2027-margin-gains-93CH-4740662). Sales for April to September were still 7% lower than a year earlier. Orders in this business come in lumps.

So in the short term, the direction is clear but the speed isn't.

## 5. The long term story: why people pay up

- **A small share of a big market.** [BCG](https://web-assets.bcg.com/45/16/f1efbcae4bb7a0a270fbfc9ab496/bcg-ipso-whitepaper-unleashing-the-tiger.pdf) sizes the global market at about $145 billion. India has only 2% to 3%, about $3 to 3.5 billion. BCG sees a potential of $22 to 25 billion by 2035. That's a potential, not a promise.
- **AI in drug discovery.** AI helps drug companies find more candidates. More candidates means more lab and scale up work. I haven't seen it in order numbers yet, but the direction makes sense.
- **Moving into harder molecules.** Indian CDMOs mostly make small molecules, chemically made drugs. The next step is biologics like peptides (the family GLP-1 obesity drugs belong to), antibody drug conjugates (targeted cancer drugs) and oligonucleotides. If that works, it's a [second S-curve](/blog/how-stock-valuations-work/#7-the-second-s-curve-when-a-mature-stock-re-rates).
- **Build, harvest, repeat.** Factory spending goes on the balance sheet and reaches profit slowly, as depreciation. A CDMO spends heavily to double capacity, fills the plant while upkeep stays small, then uses that cash to build the next one. The harvest is where profit jumps.

| Company | P/E, 28 August 2026 | ROCE, FY26 | In one line |
|---|---|---|---|
| Syngene | 51 | [10%](https://www.screener.in/company/SYNGENE/consolidated/) | Biocon group's research and manufacturing services |
| Anthem Biosciences | 85 | [30%](https://www.screener.in/company/ANTHEM/consolidated/) | Research to commercial manufacturing, listed in 2025 |

| Divi's Laboratories | 96 | [22%](https://www.screener.in/company/DIVISLAB/consolidated/) | The largest, with both custom work and its own APIs |

P/E is the 28 August price divided by FY26 earnings per share, by my calculation. ROCE is the profit made on the money used in the business.

Two things stand out. Syngene trades much lower, and its returns on capital are much lower too. And Anthem earns the highest returns of the group without being the costliest.

## 7. Anthem: what has to go right

**The plan.**
- Unit 4, Phase 1 costs [about ₹1,200 crore and should start around March 2028](https://www.multibagg.ai/market-pulse/articles/anthem-biosciences-unit-four-concall-cmw4vtzno0046xk2r83qacqb6). It's designed to match Units 1 to 3 combined, roughly doubling capacity.
- About 30% to 35% of that investment is for biology.
- Anthem has more cash than debt, so it can fund this itself.
- It had 15 commercial molecules after the July to September quarter, and 10 in Phase 3, the last stage of trials, in May.

**What's priced in.** At 85x on 28 August, Anthem was at about 58x Prabhudas Lilladher's FY28 earnings estimate and about 47x its FY29 estimate. So smooth execution till about 2028 is already in the price. On the [reverse DCF](/blog/how-stock-valuations-work/#6-work-backwards-the-reverse-dcf), 85x needs about 23% to 24% profit growth for ten years if the P/E cools to 30x.

### How long can Anthem grow fast?

Using [Damodaran's three checks for the length of fast growth](/blog/how-stock-valuations-work/#should-it-always-be-10-years):

| Check | For Anthem | Points to |
|---|---|---|
| Size against its market | About $250 million of sales in a global market of about $145 billion that is still growing | A long runway |
| Current growth and returns | Sales grew about 26% a year from FY23 to FY26, with 30% ROCE. But the first half of FY27 was 7% down | Strong, but uneven |
| Barriers to entry | Switching takes years of approvals, and customers have stayed about 12 years. But Divi's, Sai Life and China are all adding capacity too | Strong for existing molecules, weaker for new ones |

**The runway in numbers.** If Anthem's sales grew about 6 times, to roughly $1.5 billion, it would still be only about 1% of today's global market. At 20% a year, that takes about 10 years. So a 10 year runway isn't unreasonable for the business.

**What the price assumes.** At 85x, the price needs about 10 years of 24% profit growth, or about 8 years of 24% and then 5 years of slowing, if the P/E ends at 30x.

**Compare it with the industry.** BCG's 2035 potential for India works out to roughly 18% to 21% a year from 2024, by my calculation. So the price assumes Anthem grows a little faster than even the industry's good case, for about a decade. In plain words, it has to keep winning share, not just ride the wave.

**What would change the years.** Unit 4 filling on time, biology work taking off, and more molecules turning commercial would make a longer runway believable. Slow filling, a lost customer or China staying competitive would shorten it. I'd redo this every year from scratch, not just count down from 10.

**What can beat expectations**
- More Phase 3 molecules turning commercial
- Margins holding around 45% or more, above management's earlier full year guide of [38% to 40%](https://cofacto.ai/market-news/anthem-q4-fy26-earnings-call)
- Unit 4 filling quickly after 2028

**What can go wrong**
- Lumpy shipments, like the 7% fall in the first half
- A USFDA observation at a plant
- A customer's Phase 3 trial failing
- Unit 4 filling slowly

Any of these can bring the P/E down sharply, a 20% to 30% fall in the stock. A real execution failure can be [much worse](/blog/how-stock-valuations-work/#the-10-year-model-is-the-base-case-not-the-worst-case).

## How I'd approach stocks like these

This is how I think about high multiple stocks in general, not a call on any one of them.

- **If I already held for the long term,** I'd hold through the capacity build and judge it once a year, not on one quarter's shipments.
- **If I were starting fresh for 3 to 5 years,** I wouldn't put everything in at 90x to 100x. I'd buy in parts, using 10% to 15% falls from the market or a weak quarter, as long as the reasons still hold.
- **For 6 to 12 months, or for deep value,** I'd stay away. A high multiple stock gives no margin of safety over a short period.

What I'd track: full year sales growth, margin against guidance, molecules turning commercial, dependence on top customers, and Unit 4's timeline.
