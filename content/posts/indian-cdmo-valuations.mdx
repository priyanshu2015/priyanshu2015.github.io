Few business models start as much debate as CDMOs, contract development and manufacturing organisations. Mature Western CDMOs trade at about 17x to 31x earnings. Indian ones trade at prices you'd expect for fast growing software firms:

| Company | P/E, 28 August 2026 | ROCE, FY26 |
|---|---|---|
| Syngene | 51 | [10%](https://www.screener.in/company/SYNGENE/consolidated/) |
| Anthem Biosciences | 85 | [30%](https://www.screener.in/company/ANTHEM/consolidated/) |
| Sai Life Sciences | 89 | [20%](https://www.screener.in/company/SAILIFE/consolidated/) |
| Divi's Laboratories | 96 | [22%](https://www.screener.in/company/DIVISLAB/consolidated/) |

P/E is the price on 28 August divided by FY26 earnings per share, by my calculation.

Is this hype or hard economics? To answer that, I looked at the business model, the shift away from China, how factory spending shows in the accounts, and the move into complex biologics. I use the ideas from [why a high P/E can make sense](/blog/how-stock-valuations-work/). Not investment advice, and I can be wrong.

## 1. The business model: why customers stay for years

A CDMO is the R&D lab and contract factory behind big drug companies. The US or European drug company invents the molecule and owns the patent. The CDMO works out how to make it, makes batches for clinical trials, and then manufactures it at scale once it's approved.

Once a CDMO helps develop a drug and is written into the drug's USFDA filing, changing it is a big headache. The new supplier must take over the process, run stability tests, prove it makes exactly the same thing, and often wait for fresh approval. That can take [one to three years or more](https://pda.org/pda-letter-portal/home/full-article/expert-perspectives-in-km-between-sponsors-and-third-parties).

So:

- **Relationships last.** If the drug sells, supply can continue for a decade or more. At its IPO, Anthem's top customers had been with it for about 12 years on average.
- **Margins are high.** Generic drug makers fight price wars. A CDMO supplying a patented drug is hard to replace. Anthem's operating margin was about 39% in FY26 and 46% in the July to September 2026 quarter, with 30% ROCE.

The flip side: if the drug fails a trial, or the customer cuts orders, that income goes.

## 2. Two tailwinds: AI and China+1

**AI in drug discovery.** AI tools help drug companies find promising candidates faster. More candidates at the top means more lab testing and scale-up work, which is what CDMOs do. I haven't seen this in order numbers yet, but the direction makes sense.

**Moving away from China.** China+1 means drug companies keep China but add one more supplier country. [BCG](https://web-assets.bcg.com/45/16/f1efbcae4bb7a0a270fbfc9ab496/bcg-ipso-whitepaper-unleashing-the-tiger.pdf) sizes the global market at about $145 billion. India has only 2% to 3% of it, about $3 to 3.5 billion. China's share is several times larger. BCG sees a potential for India to reach $22 to 25 billion by 2035.

The push is the US [BIOSECURE Act, law since December 2025](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2025/12/the-biosecure-act-becomes-law-in-the-united-states). It restricts US government linked business with Chinese "biotech companies of concern". The name most people talk about is WuXi AppTec, a giant Chinese contractor that works for many global drug companies. Existing contracts get about five years, so the full effect lands in the early 2030s.

It's happening slowly. At a global pharma fair in October 2026, [one Indian firm said it got US orders that used to go to China, while another said Europe still prefers China](https://medicaldialogues.in/news/industry/pharma/india-gains-pharma-orders-from-china-as-western-buyers-diversify-supply-chains-cphi-firms-180836). WuXi itself grew revenue [38.6% in January to September 2026](https://www.wuxiapptec.com/news/wuxi-news/wuxi-apptec-delivers-strong-q1-q3-2026-results-and-further-raises-full-year-guidance-driven-by-greater-success-of-multiple-customers-products-and-crdmo-model).

## 3. Why Western CDMOs trade at 17x to 31x and Indian ones at 85x+

Switzerland's Lonza was at [about 31x expected 2026 earnings](https://www.ad-hoc-news.de/boerse/news/corporate-news/lonza-group-stock-eases-below-recent-high-as-investors-digest-guidance-and-valuation/70070174) and Siegfried at [about 17x past earnings](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/SFZN.SW/). They are mature, steady compounders, and they can't easily ride the wave India is riding:

- **Cost.** Hiring PhD chemists and building USFDA approved plants in Bengaluru or Hyderabad costs a fraction of what it does in Switzerland, Germany or Boston.
- **Base size.** Lonza sold [CHF 3.4 billion in just the first half of 2026](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-lonza-h1-2026-profit-gains-fail-to-lift-shares-93CH-4806064), around $8 billion a year. A $100 million contract adds about 1.3% to its sales. Anthem's FY26 sales were about ₹2,124 crore, roughly $250 million. The same contract would add about 40%.
- **Geography.** Western drug companies already use Western CDMOs for late stage biologics. What they need now is a low cost Asian base outside China.

## 4. The capex illusion, and build, harvest, repeat

Many people think spending ₹1,200 crore on a new factory crushes this year's profit. It doesn't.

Capex goes on the **balance sheet**, not the profit and loss account. It reaches profit slowly, as **depreciation**, after the factory starts. At roughly 5% to 7% a year, ₹1,200 crore is about ₹60 to 84 crore a year. Anthem's FY26 profit was about ₹592 crore.

Anthem is a good example:

- **High capital efficiency.** ROCE of about 30%, against 22% for Divi's, 20% for Sai Life and 10% for Syngene, with more cash than debt.
- **Expansion paid upfront.** Unit 4, Phase 1 costs [about ₹1,200 crore and should start around March 2028](https://www.multibagg.ai/market-pulse/articles/anthem-biosciences-unit-four-concall-cmw4vtzno0046xk2r83qacqb6). It's designed to match Units 1 to 3 combined, so roughly double the capacity. Anthem's cash pile can fund it.

Winning CDMOs grow in waves:

| Phase | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Build** | Spend heavily to double capacity. Free cash is low |
| **Harvest** | Fill the plant. Upkeep spending stays small, say 3% to 5% of revenue, so free cash and profit jump |
| **Repeat** | Once the plant is around 75% full, use that cash to build the next unit |

So today's 85x is partly the market paying now for the double sized factory of 2029. It pays off only if the harvest happens.

## 5. Moving up: from small molecules to complex biologics

Indian CDMOs have mostly made **small molecules**: chemically made drugs, like regular tablets and their active ingredients (APIs). Western giants led in **biologics**, larger and more complex medicines. That gap is closing.

About [30% to 35% of Anthem's Unit 4 Phase 1 investment is for biology](https://www.multibagg.ai/market-pulse/articles/anthem-biosciences-unit-four-concall-cmw4vtzno0046xk2r83qacqb6), with focus on newer areas:

- **Antibody drug conjugates (ADCs):** an antibody carries a cancer drug straight to the cancer cell
- **Peptides:** the technology behind GLP-1 obesity drugs
- **Oligonucleotides:** short pieces of genetic material that turn disease genes up or down
- **Sterile fill-finish:** filling injectables into vials or syringes in germ free conditions

Western grade complex science with India's cost edge could become a [second S-curve](/blog/how-stock-valuations-work/#7-the-second-s-curve-when-a-mature-stock-re-rates) for the best Indian CDMOs, and push their P/E cool-off well into the 2030s.

## 6. What's already priced in

At 85x on 28 August, Anthem was at about 58x Prabhudas Lilladher's FY28 earnings estimate and about 47x its FY29 estimate. So smooth execution till about 2028 is already in the price. On the [reverse DCF](/blog/how-stock-valuations-work/#6-work-backwards-the-reverse-dcf), 85x needs about 23% to 24% profit growth for ten years.

**What can beat expectations**

- More late stage molecules turning commercial. Anthem had 10 in Phase 3 in May, and 15 commercial molecules after the July to September quarter
- Margins holding around 45% to 50% as complex biologics grow. Management's earlier full year guide was [38% to 40%](https://cofacto.ai/market-news/anthem-q4-fy26-earnings-call)

**What can go wrong**

- Lumpy shipments. April to September 2026 sales were 7% lower than a year earlier
- A USFDA observation at a plant
- A customer's Phase 3 trial failing
- Unit 4 filling slowly after March 2028

Any of these can bring the P/E down sharply, a 20% to 30% fall in the stock. A real execution failure can be [much worse](/blog/how-stock-valuations-work/#the-10-year-model-is-the-base-case-not-the-worst-case).

## How I'd approach high multiple stocks like these

This is how I think about such stocks in general, not a call on any one of them.

- **If I already held for the long term,** I'd hold through the capacity build and judge it once a year, not on one quarter's shipments.
- **If I were starting fresh for 3 to 5 years,** I wouldn't put everything in at 90x to 100x. I'd buy in parts, using 10% to 15% falls from the market or a weak quarter, as long as the reasons still hold.
- **For 6 to 12 months, or for deep value,** I'd stay away. A high multiple compounder gives no margin of safety over a short period.
