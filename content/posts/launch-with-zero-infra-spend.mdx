When you're starting out, every rupee you spend on servers is a rupee you don't spend on the product. We took Prysm from 0 to 30K monthly visits without paying for infrastructure in the early days. It wasn't a trick. We just used the free credits and free tiers that cloud companies already give to new builders, one after another.

I made a [short reel about this](https://www.instagram.com/p/DTvPYUkjdDV/). This post is the detailed version, with links.

> Credit amounts and rules change often. Everything below is correct as far as I know at the time of writing, but always check the official page before you plan around a number.

## The idea: stack credits in order

Don't apply for everything on day one. Credits expire, usually within a year. Use them in this order so they last as long as possible:

1. **Free trial credits** while you build and test.
2. **Startup program credits** once you have a real product and a website.
3. **Free-forever tiers** for the parts that never need to be paid for.

## Step 1: Free trial credits

These are for new accounts and need almost nothing to get.

| Provider | What you get | Link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Google Cloud | $300 in credits for new customers, plus 20+ products with a free usage limit | [cloud.google.com/free](https://cloud.google.com/free) |
| AWS | New accounts get $100 in credits, up to $200 as you try core services, on a free plan for up to 6 months | [aws.amazon.com/free](https://aws.amazon.com/free) |

Use this phase to build and test. Don't run production traffic on a trial if you can avoid it, because when it ends you'll be migrating in a hurry.

## Step 2: Startup programs

Once you have a working product, a website, and ideally a company page on LinkedIn, apply to these. Most don't need investors.

- **AWS Activate.** You get $1,000 in credits as a self-funded startup, even before you are registered. Once your startup has a DPIIT recognition certificate, you can apply for $4,000 more. [aws.amazon.com/activate](https://aws.amazon.com/activate/)
- **Google for Startups Cloud Program.** You get $2,000 in credits as an early, pre-funded startup, and much larger tiers open up once you're funded or building with AI. [cloud.google.com/startup](https://cloud.google.com/startup)
- **Microsoft for Startups.** Azure credits that unlock in levels as you go, plus other tools. No investor needed. [startups.microsoft.com](https://startups.microsoft.com)
- **Vercel for Startups.** Credits for hosting your frontend on Vercel, reported at up to $30,000 for eligible startups. [Apply here](https://vercel.com/startups/credits)
- **Cloudflare for Startups.** Credits for Cloudflare's developer platform, with smaller tiers for bootstrapped startups. [cloudflare.com/forstartups](https://www.cloudflare.com/forstartups/)

Two tips from experience:

- **Write a clear one-paragraph description** of what you're building and who it's for. A vague application is the most common reason I've seen people get rejected.
- **Use your company email and domain**, not a Gmail address. It makes you look like a real company, because you are one.

## Step 3: Free platforms for each part of your stack

Here's how the pieces fit. Some of these are free forever within limits, so you never have to pay for them while you're small.

| Part | What I'd use | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frontend | [Vercel](https://vercel.com) or [Cloudflare Pages](https://pages.cloudflare.com) | Push to GitHub and it's live. Vercel's free Hobby plan is for non-commercial use, so once you're a company, [apply for Vercel startup credits](https://vercel.com/startups/credits). |
| Database | [Neon](https://neon.com) | Serverless Postgres with a free plan. It scales to zero when nobody is using it, so a side project or early product costs nothing. |
| Backend | AWS or Google Cloud, on credits | Servers and managed databases (like Amazon RDS) once you outgrow free tiers, paid for by the credits from Steps 1 and 2. |
| Caching | A managed Redis on your cloud credits | Cache expensive queries and AI answers. It saves money and makes the app faster. |
| File storage | [Cloudflare R2](https://www.cloudflare.com/developer-platform/products/r2/) | 10 GB free every month and no egress fees, so serving files doesn't surprise you with a bill. |
| AI models | [Gemini API](https://ai.google.dev) free tier, then cloud credits | Good enough to prototype AI features without paying per call on day one. |
| Building | [Claude Code](https://claude.com/claude-code) | Not free, but it's how a small team ships at the speed of a bigger one. |
| Domain | Any registrar; [Cloudflare Registrar](https://www.cloudflare.com/products/registrar/) sells at cost | The one thing you will probably pay for. It's worth it. |

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Set billing alerts on day one.** Every provider lets you set a budget alert. One forgotten GPU or a runaway loop can burn through credits in a weekend.
- **Know your expiry dates.** Put them in a calendar. Plan the switch to the next set of credits before the current ones run out.
- **Don't lock yourself in.** Credits on one cloud are tempting, but keep your app in containers and use standard databases, so moving later is a weekend, not a month.
- **Credits don't cover everything.** Marketplace and third-party services usually aren't included. Read what's excluded.
- **Free doesn't mean unlimited.** Free tiers have usage limits. Watch them as you grow.

## When to start paying

Pay when it buys you something you need: reliability for paying customers, support, or time. By then you should have revenue or funding to cover it. Credits are there to get you to that point, not to replace a business model.
